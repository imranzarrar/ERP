Three graphic directions, side by side

Storefront (the last version) was intentionally restrained — a clean admin panel. These three lean further into visual personality, each a genuinely different bet rather than a palette swap on the same template. Same content throughout (June sales KPIs, three invoice rows) so the only variable is the design language itself. Two of the app's three planned languages (Arabic, Urdu) are RTL, so every direction below is built on logical CSS (start/end, not left/right) — toggle below to see it actually mirror, not just hear it claimed.

Direction A

Ticket stock

Built from the physical object retail already runs on — the receipt. Perforated stub edges, register-tape monospace, a hand-stamped “paid”/“due” mark tilted like a real ink stamp.

Direction B

Boutique

For the higher end of retail — a jewel-tone header panel, a serif display face carrying the numbers themselves (KPIs read like a menu of figures, not a spreadsheet), a metallic rule instead of a hairline. Ivory cards break from the dark panel the way a price card sits inside a boutique window.

Direction C

Marketplace

The most colorful of the three — each KPI gets its own hue rather than one shared accent, the way stalls at a market each carry their own signage. Rounded, friendly, initials-in-a-circle for customers instead of plain text. Best fit if the product serves many small, varied retailers rather than one large account.

How to read these: A is the most tactile/nostalgic (works well if the brand voice is craft/heritage); B is the most premium/quiet (works if margins and average order value matter more than volume); C is the most energetic/varied (works best across many small, dissimilar retailers). None of these are full app shells yet — each is a fast read on personality only. Pick one (or name what to recombine from more than one) and I'll build it out into the full sidebar + list + print-document system next.

On RTL: the Arabic strings above are illustrative placeholders for layout-proofing, not verified professional financial terminology — whatever ships would go through a real translator. What matters here is structural: every layout uses flex/grid with logical alignment, so flipping dir mirrors the whole panel automatically, no separate RTL stylesheet needed. The one bug this caught: Marketplace's status pill used a hardcoded margin-left, which would've stuck to the wrong side under Arabic/Urdu — fixed to a logical margin-inline-start before you even asked, now proven by the toggle rather than just promised.
